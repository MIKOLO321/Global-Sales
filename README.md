## Global Sales Executive Intelligence 

### Business Problem Statement

The company has plenty of sales data related to products, customers, countries, and various time periods. Yet, the information provided is not structured and organized to help analyze general business performance and to highlight those areas that influence revenue and profitability of the company in the most significant way.

The main problem here is that it is necessary to analyze existing sales data and to see what areas and products perform well, what areas and products generate maximum profitability, what is the dynamics of sales, etc., and what aspects should be paid attention to by the management.

The current project involves using of Power BI software for transforming raw sales data into an interactive business intelligence dashboard. The analysis will include sales data, product costs, gross profits, profit margins, customer data, geographical performance, etc.

The goal of the project is to provide management with data-driven information that can support better product decisions, market expansion, profitability improvement, and future business planning.

#### Tools Used
* Microsoft Excel
* Microsoft Power BI
* Power Query
* DAX

 ### Key Findings
#### 1. High Profitability
The firm made $28.41M in revenue and $11.70M in gross profits, posting a gross profit margin of about 41.2% overall.

#### 2. Revenue Focus on Certain Countries
The Australia and the United States generate about 62.5% of the total revenue of the company. They are the major sources of revenue for the firm.

#### 3. Canada is a Country that Should be Focused On
Although Canada makes up a smaller percentage of the total revenue of the company, it has the highest country gross profit margin of about 42.1% and therefore has the potential to be a profitable market.
#### 4. High-Value Products
The Mountain-200 line of products has high sales together with high margins of about 46%. It is one of the best product lines in terms of profitability.
#### 5. Being the Major Revenue Producer Does Not Mean a High Margin
While the R line of products generates the most sales, its margin of about 38.3% is much lower than the M line that has a margin of about 45.9%.
#### 6. Business Growth over Years
From 2005 to 2008, the sales grew from about $2.93M to $9.67M and gross profit from $1.18M to $4.01M.

#### Strategic Recommendations
#### 1. Emphasize Profitable Growth
Evaluate products and markets using revenue, gross profit and gross margin measures to facilitate profitable growth.
#### 2. Solidify Core Markets
Maintain investment in core markets of Australia and United States through customer retention, targeted marketing and product availability due to their significant share in overall revenue.
#### 3. Expand Canada Market
Explore ways to expand sales in Canada without compromising on its high gross profit margin through customer acquisition and market-specific strategies.
#### 4. Prioritize High-Margin Products
Focus efforts on inventory, marketing and promotions on the product ranges such as Mountain-200 range and M products which are performing well in sales and profitability.
#### 5. Enhance Low-Margin Product Performance
Explore ways to reduce production cost and prices for the low-margin products such as R and T products so that they can be more profitable without necessarily reducing sales.
#### 6. Use Historical Data Trends for Future Forecasting
Utilize historical trends of sales and profit in making decisions about future inventory, marketing and expansion plans, as well as investigating the growth slowdown at the end of analyzed period.
